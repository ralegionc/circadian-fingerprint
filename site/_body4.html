One timezone

Asking the question properly

There is one way to get a real answer about three in the morning, and it is to pick an edition where three in the morning exists. Japanese Wikipedia is close to ideal: its readers are overwhelmingly in one country, Japan keeps a single timezone, and it has observed no daylight saving since 1951. Add nine hours to UTC and you have the clock its readers were actually looking at.

So the pipeline was pointed at ja.wikipedia as well: the top 1,000 articles of each sample day, the same four weeks of hourly dumps, the same median-across-days method, the same index against the edition's own baseline. 3,007 articles cleared the filters, across 27 full JST days.

The quiet window immediately moves to where it belongs. On English Wikipedia the six quietest hours were 06:00 to 11:00 UTC, the middle of the night in Chicago and the middle of the morning in Delhi. On Japanese Wikipedia they are 02:00 to 07:00 JST, the middle of the night for essentially every reader.

The afternoon column is not a grab bag. Seventeen of the top twenty-four are sumo: wrestlers, a commentator, the list of tournament champions and the article on the prize banners carried around the ring. The September grand tournament ran from the 14th to the 28th, inside the window. The top division fights late in the afternoon and the broadcast finishes around six, and fourteen of those seventeen peak at 17:00 or 18:00 JST, eleven of them at 18:00 exactly: the hour people reach for a phone to look up the wrestler they have just watched.

Nothing in the pipeline knows what sumo is, when the tournament was held, or which of 3,007 article titles are wrestlers. It has hourly counts and nothing else, and it reconstructed a broadcast schedule from them. That is the same trick as Thursday Night Football in the previous section, run on a different continent against a different clock.

The small hours are the other half of the answer, and they are blunt. Reading down the thirty articles most over-represented between 2am and 7am, the great majority are adult-industry subjects, studios, platforms and performers, with late-night anime and idol groups making up most of the rest. It is the strongest single topical signal anywhere in this project, and it is invisible on English Wikipedia, where 3am is not a time of day so much as a region of the world.

Which is the whole point. Japanese Wikipedia in the afternoon is a second screen for the television. At four in the morning it is something else. The only thing standing between English Wikipedia and the same answer is that its readers do not share a clock.
