Longitude

What gets read at three in the morning

This is the question the whole exercise is really for. Wikipedia's hourly API will not answer it, since per-article data stops at daily granularity, so the numbers below come from the raw hourly dumps: all four weeks of them, filtered down to a watchlist of the 4,250 articles that reached the daily top 1,000 at some point in September.

Each article's curve is the median across days of its own daily shape, indexed against the curve of the whole watchlist. Taking the median matters more than it sounds: a first pass without it returned Teachers' Day and a run of US Open players, articles with one enormous day whose single burst set the entire profile. Anything with more than 30% of its views on one day is now dropped outright. 3,100 articles survive.

And the answer, for English Wikipedia, is that the question is broken.

The left-hand column is not a list of nocturnal subjects. It is the Philippines, India, Vietnam, Thailand and Australia. The right-hand column is the United States and Britain. At 09:00 UTC the Americas are asleep and South Asia is wide awake, so an article's hour-of-day profile on a globally read edition is mostly a readout of where its readers live. The clock is measuring longitude.

There is no fix available inside English Wikipedia. There is no timezone to convert to, because the readers do not share one.

The axis that survives

Tuesday is Tuesday everywhere

Day of week has none of the hour's ambiguity. Timezones shift the clock; they do not shift the calendar. Whatever longitude a reader sits at, Saturday arrives for them on Saturday, and the weekday axis is therefore the one place in this data where a difference between two articles is a difference about the articles.

It works. Sorting the same 3,100 articles by weekend index separates them cleanly into sport and entertainment at one end and news, institutions and reference at the other, with no topic labels supplied anywhere in the pipeline.

The cleanest check that the method works is an article that names its own answer. Thursday Night Football sits near the bottom of the weekend ranking with an index of 0.20. Its daily curve reads 0.38, 0.45, 0.50, 1.93, 3.22, 0.25, 0.27 from Monday to Sunday: a spike on Thursday, a bigger one on Friday as people look up what happened, and nothing at the weekend. Nobody told the pipeline when the game is played. It recovered the schedule from timestamps alone.

The same axis picks out working tools without being asked. ChatGPT comes in at 0.69, elevated Tuesday through Thursday and down on both weekend days, which is the shape of something people use at a desk.
